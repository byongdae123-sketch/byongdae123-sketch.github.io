RESEARCH / AREAS OF INQUIRY

Research

My scholarship centers on East Asian ethics and intellectual history, particularly the philosophical vocabulary through which moral cultivation, authority, and practical judgment have been understood.

01

Korean Neo-Confucianism

Joseon intellectual history is at the center of my research. I study the ways classical concepts of moral agency and self-cultivation were interpreted, debated, and enacted, with attention to both philosophical argument and the historical lives of texts.

Joseon thoughtSelf-cultivationConfucian moral philosophy
02

Comparative East Asian Thought

My comparative work considers the intellectual relationships among Korean, Chinese, and Japanese traditions. Rather than assuming that similar terms have identical meanings, I attend to the differing contexts in which classical concepts acquire philosophical force.

Classical interpretationChina · Korea · JapanIntellectual exchange
03

Ethics and Moral Education

My work also considers how traditions of practical wisdom and ethical formation illuminate moral education. I am interested in the relationship between theoretical commitments and the ordinary practices through which character and judgment are cultivated.

Moral agencyEthical educationPractical judgment
04

Religion and Philosophical Authority

A related area of inquiry concerns Heaven, transcendence, and religious authority in East Asian thought, especially the limits of what interpreters and moral agents may claim to know or judge.

Heaven (天)Religious thoughtAuthority and judgment
